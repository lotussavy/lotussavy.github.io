<section class="about-hero">
  <h1>About Kamal Acharya</h1>
  <p>
    I am a <strong>Postdoctoral Research Associate in Mechanical Engineering at <a href="https://www.baylor.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Baylor University</a></strong>
    and a member of the <a href="https://avia.research.baylor.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">AVIA (Advanced Vehicle Intelligence and Autonomy) Lab</a>.
    My research combines advanced air mobility, neurosymbolic AI, forecasting, and optimization to
    develop interpretable decision-support systems for future transportation.
  </p>
</section>

<section class="about-section">
  <h2>Postdoctoral Appointment</h2>
  <p>
    Since August 2026, I have worked with <a href="https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=zvMPg9gAAAAJ&amp;hl=en&amp;oi=ao" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Dr. Liang Sun</a>
    in Baylor's AVIA Lab at the intersection of aerospace engineering and artificial intelligence.
    My work explores how machine learning, optimization, control, and uncertainty-aware decision
    methods can support advanced mobility and autonomous aerospace systems.
  </p>
  <p>
    At Baylor, I am building on my background in mobility-demand modeling and trustworthy AI while
    expanding my research in intelligent and autonomous aerospace systems. I contribute through
    computational experiments, research software and datasets, scholarly publications, collaborative
    proposals, and student mentorship.
  </p>
</section>

<section class="about-section">
  <h2>Research Focus</h2>
  <p>
    My work sits at the intersection of Advanced Air Mobility demand modeling, neurosymbolic AI,
    forecasting, and optimization. I am interested in AI systems that are not only accurate, but also
    transparent enough for planners, researchers, and decision-makers to understand and use.
  </p>
  <p>
    My research includes demand forecasting for regional and urban air mobility, interpretable travel
    demand prediction using symbolic rules and neural models, and neural-accelerated optimization for
    pre-disaster mobility planning. At Baylor, I am extending this foundation toward aerospace autonomy,
    intelligent coordination, and decision-making under uncertainty.
  </p>
</section>

<section class="about-section">
  <h2>My Academic Journey</h2>
  <p>
    I earned my Ph.D. in Information Systems from the <a href="https://umbc.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">University of Maryland, Baltimore County (UMBC)</a>,
    where I studied data-driven learning and neurosymbolic AI for Advanced Air Mobility demand modeling.
    Earlier work in computing, engineering, teaching, and transportation research shaped my interest in
    connecting rigorous AI methods with consequential real-world planning problems.
  </p>
  <p>
    Transportation systems are becoming more data-rich, automated, and interconnected. What drives my
    work is the opportunity to make these systems safer and more useful by creating models that account
    for uncertainty, infrastructure constraints, policy choices, and human trust—not predictive accuracy alone.
  </p>
</section>
