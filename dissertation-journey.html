PhD dissertation · UMBC · 2026

Enhancing Demand Modeling for Advanced Air Mobility Using Data-Driven Learning and Neurosymbolic AI

How can we forecast demand for a transportation system that has little historical operating data—while keeping the predictions behaviorally meaningful, accurate, and interpretable?

Kamal Acharya · Doctor of Philosophy in Information Systems · Advisor: Dr. Houbing Song · Co-advisor: Dr. Alvaro Velasquez

01 · DISCOVER

From a forecasting problem to a research program

Advanced Air Mobility spans urban airport connections and regional trips. The dissertation addresses three linked gaps rather than treating demand prediction as an isolated machine-learning problem.

CHALLENGE 1

Limited observations

Mature passenger-use records for emerging air services do not yet exist. Travel choices must be estimated from related behavioral and spatial evidence.

CHALLENGE 2

Behavior and geography

Adoption varies with travel time, monetary cost, perceived risk, reliability, trip purpose and location.

CHALLENGE 3

Model transparency

Black-box predictions can be difficult to explain or evaluate when decision-makers need defensible infrastructure and policy assessments.

02 · COMPARE

Three complementary dissertation contributions

Select a research strand to see its question, method, case studies, and role in the overall dissertation.

When does air travel become attractive?

Method: Generalized Cost of Trip combines monetary fares, time valuation, reliability and perceived risk. Settings: regional travel in Tennessee and urban airport-access trips in New York City.

Contribution: Makes demand estimates sensitive to traveler trade-offs and local travel conditions rather than treating distance alone as the adoption mechanism.

Explore the Tennessee study →

How can spatial flows and planning searches be modeled better?

Methods: machine-learning-enhanced gravity modeling and neural-network-accelerated genetic optimization. These are distinct contributions: gravity models estimate flows, while optimization searches for feasible planning choices.

Contribution: Captures nonlinear spatial interactions and explores how learning can assist optimization without replacing its planning constraints.

Explore the gravity model study →

How can a predictive model make its reasoning inspectable?

Method: decision-tree-derived symbolic rules are incorporated into a neural-network-based demand prediction approach.

Contribution: Connects flexible learned representations with explicit, inspectable rules, addressing interpretability and robustness under data sparsity.

03 · EXPLAIN

Try the travel-choice trade-off

Adjust trip prices, travel times and value of time. The calculator illustrates the logic of generalized trip cost; it is not a calibrated dissertation model, reported experiment, or adoption forecast.

—

Illustrative formula: generalized cost = monetary cost + (time in hours × value of time) + optional penalty. Ground penalty is set to $0 for this teaching example. Real adoption depends on additional behavioral assumptions and empirical calibration; a lower generalized cost does not establish actual market share.
