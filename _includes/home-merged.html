Background

A little about my path

My work has moved from engineering and information systems toward transportation research that connects prediction with decision-making.

I am part of the Advanced Vehicle Intelligence and Autonomy (AVIA) Lab at Baylor University. Before joining Baylor, I completed my PhD in Information Systems at the University of Maryland, Baltimore County (UMBC), where I studied demand modeling for emerging air mobility services.

Teaching has also been an important part of my academic career. I have more than a decade of university teaching experience and have mentored students on research and engineering projects.

Current research

Planning the operation of eVTOL fleets

Moving from the question of where demand exists to how future services can respond.

Scheduling aircraft under real operating constraints

Imagine several vertiports, a limited fleet of electric aircraft, and passenger requests arriving throughout the day. An aircraft must have enough energy to complete a trip and retain a safe reserve; charging and vehicle availability influence the next assignment.

My ongoing work at Baylor studies how optimization and simulation can help evaluate these interconnected decisions. The focus includes rolling-horizon scheduling and the trade-offs between service, energy use, and operational feasibility.

This is ongoing research. Detailed methods and performance results will be shared when ready for public release.
