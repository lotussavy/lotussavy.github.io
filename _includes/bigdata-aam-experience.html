01 · The modeling pipeline

An air journey is more than a flight

The study uses Tennessee census-tract employment origin–destination flows to identify potential passenger trips. For AAM, the entire travel chain matters: reach an airport, fly between airports, and continue by ground to the destination.

02 · Explore generalized cost

What makes the complete AAM journey competitive?

Generalized Cost of Trip (GCT) combines direct travel expenses, the monetary value of travel time, and risk-related costs. This is an illustrative calculator built to explain the paper's decomposition; it does not rerun the published regressions or produce actual demand forecasts.

Positive-magnitude teaching equation

All calculator inputs are hypothetical. The real study estimated mode-specific cost, time, and risk models using external datasets; no published route-level or performance numbers are inferred from these controls. AAM access, fares, weather, capacity and demand behavior may alter feasibility.

03 · Interpreting published findings

Where does the model find favorable AAM conditions?

The paper's abstract reports two prominent patterns from its Tennessee experiments. Explore the criteria as research findings—not a deterministic flight-booking rule.

Longer trip distances

The study reports that trips longer than roughly 250 miles are more likely to be viable for AAM under its modeling conditions. This does not mean every trip above 250 miles should switch to air.
