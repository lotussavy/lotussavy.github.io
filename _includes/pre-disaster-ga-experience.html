Smarter Search Before the Storm

When a weather disaster approaches, airports must prepare additional outbound flights without unnecessarily disrupting routine operations. Explore how a neural network supplies promising destination-airport choices to a genetic algorithm, why a smaller search can produce comparable plans, and how airport capacity affects the selection.

01 · Understanding the hybrid optimizer

A neural network gives the GA a head start

A standard genetic algorithm starts from a candidate population, evaluates fitness, then applies selection, crossover, and mutation. The proposed method supplements that population with NN-generated destination selections before the GA refines the solutions.

Traditional Genetic Algorithm

A GA searches combinations of ten candidate destinations with crossover and mutation. Its stronger standalone test used 75 individuals and 25 generations.

Neural network trained for 5 epochs → 10 binary destination indicators → introduced into GA population

The NN is a selection predictor (31 inputs and 10 outputs, with two hidden layers of 64 neurons). It is not described in the paper as predicting fitness scores; that is proposed as future work.

Cross-airport transfer: Daytona Beach (DAB)

The NN's synthetic training data excluded DAB, while the evaluation tested evacuation schedules originating at DAB. The reported similar choices support a limited cross-airport generalization finding within the experiment, not guaranteed transfer to any new airport or disaster.
